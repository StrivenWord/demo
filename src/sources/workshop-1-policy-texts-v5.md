# Workshop 1 Policy Texts

Sixteen policy texts. Four employees, four flavors each. Plain language. No em dashes. Parallel structure held across all four variants of each employee.

## Employee 1: Lux, DSS Caseworker

**The scenario.** Lux is a caseworker at the Herkimer County Department of Social Services conducting a HEAP heating assistance intake. She runs the applicant's information through the county's AI screening tool and receives a preliminary eligibility assessment. The assessment looks right to her. Before she can finish the case, she has to leave for the day. She emails the applicant's case summary -- name, household composition, income data, case history -- to her personal email account so she can finish the intake from home that evening.

**The violation question:** Did Lux violate the policy?

---

### Employee 1 / Green: Guardrails
Security Hi | Accountability Hi | Efficiency Lo | Innovation Lo

The caseworker may use the county's designated AI screening tool to generate a preliminary eligibility or risk assessment during a benefits intake. The tool must appear on the county's approved AI tool list. Client data entered into or generated by the tool must not leave county systems. The caseworker may not transmit client information -- including case summaries, household data, income records, or AI-generated assessments -- to any personal account, personal device, or outside server. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

Before any case is moved forward based on an AI-generated output, the assessment must be reviewed and confirmed by a supervisor or a second caseworker. The caseworker documents the AI tool used, the date, and the outcome of the supervisor review in the case record.

The caseworker may not adopt, download, or pilot any AI tool not on the county's approved list. Tool selection is centralized.

Every AI-assisted intake interaction is logged with the caseworker's staff ID, the tool name, and a timestamp. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days. A named caseworker is responsible for every determination. The AI assessment is an input to that determination; the caseworker, not the AI tool, makes the determination.

## Employee 2: Puk, Sheriff's Deputy

**The scenario.** Puk is on patrol on a rural stretch of Route 28. He stops a vehicle with a partially obscured rear plate. His mobile data terminal is showing a connection dropout. He photographs the plate with his department-issued phone running an approved AI image recognition tool. The tool returns a match and a record flag -- the registered owner has an outstanding warrant. Puk decides to proceed with the stop based on the AI result alone. He does not call dispatch to confirm.

**The violation question:** Did Puk violate the policy?

---

### Employee 2 / Green: Guardrails
Security Hi | Accountability Hi | Efficiency Lo | Innovation Lo

The deputy may use only department-issued, security-vetted AI tools for field identification tasks including plate recognition. The tool must appear on the department's approved list and must comply with CJIS security requirements. Criminal justice data processed by the tool must not leave county or state-authorized systems. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

The deputy may not act directly on an AI-generated record flag without first confirming the result through an independent channel -- dispatch, a backup terminal, or a second officer. The AI output is a preliminary indicator only. The deputy proceeds based on confirmed information, not the AI result alone.

The deputy may not adopt, download, or pilot any AI tool not on the department's approved list. Tool selection is centralized.

Every AI-assisted field query is logged with the deputy's badge number, the tool name, and a timestamp. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days. The deputy is the named responsible officer for each stop. The AI tool result is an input to the deputy's field judgment; the deputy, not the AI tool, makes the determination.

## Employee 3: Jam, Public Health Nurse

**The scenario.** Jam is a public health nurse at Herkimer County Public Health. She opens the county's AI prioritization tool on Tuesday morning and follows its ranked schedule for the entire day -- six home visits and two inspections, in the order the tool assigned. She does not log that she used the tool. She does not review the ranking with her supervisor before starting her day.

**The violation question:** Did Jam violate the policy?

---

### Employee 3 / Green: Guardrails
Security Hi | Accountability Hi | Efficiency Lo | Innovation Lo

The nurse may use the county's designated AI prioritization tool to receive a ranked caseload for the day. The tool must be county-procured and appear on the county's approved AI tool list. Resident health information processed by the tool must not leave county systems. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

The nurse may not implement the AI-ranked schedule without first reviewing it with her supervisor. The supervisor confirms or adjusts the day's priorities before the nurse begins her rounds. The nurse documents that supervisor review occurred and notes any departures from the AI ranking.

The nurse may not adopt or pilot any AI tool not on the county's approved list. Tool selection is centralized.

Every use of the AI prioritization tool is logged with the nurse's staff ID, the tool name, and a timestamp. Logs are retained for a minimum of 90 days. The supervisor is the named responsible party for the confirmed daily schedule. The AI ranking is an input to that schedule; the nurse and supervisor, not the AI tool, determine the day's priorities.

---

The nurse may act directly on the AI-generated ranking and organize her day accordingly, without a separate supervisor confirmation step. The nurse applies her clinical judgment and may reorder visits where her professional assessment differs from the AI output.

The nurse is encouraged to identify and pilot new AI tools within defined parameters. Tools that meet county security and operational standards may be adopted.

## Employee 4: Wow, Administration Staffer

**The scenario.** Wow is an administrative staffer in the Herkimer County Attorney's office preparing a formal SNAP benefits denial notice. She uses a commercial AI drafting tool she found online -- not the county's designated tool -- to generate the draft. She reviews the draft, thinks it looks good, and routes it for the attorney's signature the same afternoon without verifying the regulatory citations or the appeal deadline against the source documents, without logging the AI use, and without supervisor sign-off before routing.

**The violation question:** Did Wow violate the policy?

---

### Employee 4 / Green: Guardrails
Security Hi | Accountability Hi | Efficiency Lo | Innovation Lo

The staffer may use the county's designated AI drafting tool to generate a draft benefits denial notice. The tool must appear on the county's approved AI tool list. Applicant information and case data must not leave county systems; the staffer may not use any tool that transmits legal work product or applicant information to an outside server or third party. All approved tools are auditable by IT.

The staffer may not route the draft notice for signature without supervisor sign-off. Before routing, the staffer verifies every factual claim, regulatory citation, deadline, and appeal rights statement against the source documents. The AI tool cannot supply facts, deadlines, or citations; the staffer confirms each element independently. The routing slip notes that AI was used in drafting and that verification is complete.

The staffer may not adopt or pilot any AI drafting tool not on the county's approved list. Tool selection is centralized.

Every AI-assisted drafting session is logged with the staffer's staff ID, the tool name, and a timestamp. The log is retained as part of the determination record. A named staffer is responsible for the accuracy of the notice. The AI draft is an input; the staffer, not the AI tool, is responsible for the determination.
